# UI — Content area (views, panels, tool cards)

> **Scope.** Everything inside the dockview: the 3D views with their toolbar and floating tool cards, and the session panels (Statistics, the well log, …). The shell around it — the top bar, the import door, the shared components and the charter — is on [[UI — Shell, Widgets and Charter|UI-Drawer-Toolbar-Shared]]; the data each panel shows is computed by the modules on [[Engine — Dispatchers|Engine-Dispatchers]].
>
> **The one pattern.** A panel or a card is a **widget**: a Vue component in `widgets/<name>/vue-components/src`, bundled into `widgets/<name>/trame_fespp_<name>/module/serve`, wrapped by a `trame` element class in `widgets/<name>/trame_fespp_<name>/widgets`. The Python side of a panel (`app/views/<kind>_panel.py`) is a **mount**: ~30 lines that bind state vars and controllers to the widget's props and events and draw nothing themselves. There is no Vuetify in a panel or a card since 2026-09-16; the last Python-drawn UI is the top bar and the docked trees' frame.

## 1. The dockview (`MultiView`)

`MultiView(server)` subclasses `trame.widgets.dockview.DockView` and is placed once in the shell. It owns:

- **The registry** `{panel_id → pv_view}` (3D views only) and `{panel_id → html_view}` (every panel's `VtkRemoteView` when it has one), plus `kind_of(panel_id)`, `panel_ids_of_kind(kind)`, `view_ids()`. `server.context.multi_view` is how the engine's fan-out (`RenderScheduler`, `panel_resolver`) reaches the live views; `controller.view_update` is never overwritten.
- **`add_view(direction, kind, reference_panel_id)`**: builds the panel's content through its kind's builder inside a `DivLayout` template, registers the 3D view with `controller.wire_view` (which seeds the per-view state and makes the view's `PdcScene`), inherits the reference view's eyes when the kind says so (`inherit_eyes`, the Globe), adds the dockview panel (split `right` / `below` the reference, else a tab), frames the camera on what is loaded and pushes the first frame.
- **Close** (`remove_panel` → `_on_view_closed` → `controller.on_panel_closed`): the panel's per-view state, listeners, isolates and camera links go; a recycled id never inherits a dead one's.
- **Layout persistence**: the last layout dockview reported is restored on reload, so the panels come back where they were.
- **Camera links**: each 3D view's `VtkRemoteView` binds its mouse release to `controller.camera_link_sync`; `_sync_camera_from(panel_id)` copies the camera to every linked view (`view_links` state, symmetric).
- **`fespp_render_panels`**: the list `[{id, title, globe}]` of open 3D views and the Globe (`globe` true), what the Link cameras and Slicers ("Copy from…") cards read; the Globe is left out of both.

## 2. A 3D view (`ViewPanel`) and its tool cards

```
┌──┬──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│tb│  render (VtkRemoteView, one per panel)                        │
│  │   ┌ Data tree ─────┐   ┌ Colormap · PORO ───┐            │
│▣ │   │ tree widget         │   │ colormap widget     │            │
│◐ │   │ (ticks, eyes, ▾)    │   └─────────────────────┘            │
│⛶ │   └─────────────────────┘        right-click → ViewContextMenu │
└──┴──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- **The toolbar** (`widgets/view-tools`, `ViewToolbar`): one button per tool the view **offers** — `view_tools_<vid>`, rewritten by `boot._refresh_view_tools` from `TOOLS` and the view's kind (a 3D view or the Globe: `kinds`) and the data (`needs`: `grid` = the view has an IJK grid, `volume` = something with cells that have a volume). A button that could only open an empty card is dropped, not greyed. The three **mode** buttons (`MODE_TOOLS`: pick on the surface, frustum through the volume, cursor position) flip a per-view mode var instead of opening a card.
- **The cards** (`widgets/view-card`, `FloatingCard`): every tool's card is built at panel creation (a view flips modes at runtime) and shown by `ui_open_tools_by_view[vid]`. They are panels of a dock of the view's own (`widgets/dock`, `FpDock` with the class `fp-cards`, `view_panel._right`): the render is its fixed centre (`center`, a panel whose tab row is hidden), each open card a panel (`panels`, made from `ui_card_specs__<vid>`); the render and the cards are mounted once in a hidden reservoir and moved into their panel's slot while it exists (`FpDockSlot`, `FloatingCard in_panel`), so a closed card is hidden, not rebuilt. A card closed from its panel asks the server (`panel_close_request` → `close_tool`); reopened, it comes back at its last place, which the dock remembers. Where the cards stand is `ui_card_layout__<vid>` (dockview's own layout, read once when the dock is ready, saved with the session). A card stays in its view. `FESPP_CARD_DOCK=0` gives the floating cards back (draggable, dockable left, right or bottom, `ui_card_geometry__<vid>`) until that mode is removed.
- **The buttons of a tab row** (`FpDock header_buttons`, `FpDockHeader`): every group's tab row, views and cards alike, ends with dock left, right, top, bottom, detach and close (`view_chrome.HEADER_BUTTONS`), acting on the group's active panel; never on the cards' centre. The dock does them itself (`addGroup({direction})` at the root's edge, then `moveTo`; `addFloatingGroup` centred at 60 %; back from floating to the place recorded before), except those listed in `header_emit`, sent as `header_action {id, action, width, height, floating}`: the views' dock sends `float` to `view_float`, which detaches and anchors on the server (`MultiView.detach_panel`, `anchor_panel`). Each button carries `data-fp-action`. A view's own chrome sits between its tab and these buttons, centred (`view_chrome.chrome_place`, `chrome_tools`). The frame carries the **title** and the **target** — what the tool acts on, written by the host (`_set_tool_target(vid, key, text)` → `ui_tool_target__<vid>[key]`: "Slicers · Geogrid", "Attributes · Facies", "Colormap · PORO"). The widget inside draws no second header.
- **Default cards**: the Data tree opens with a plain 3D view once there is data (`_on_has_data`); the Globe view opens with its own card only.
- **Thresholds on an unstructured grid, not only an IjkGrid** (2026-09-23): the pre-BlockScope engine thresholded BOTH kinds — `RepInScene.add_threshold` / `threshold_dispatch` / `rendered_sources` are that generic layer, still in the tree. The BlockScope port carried the IJK half over (the `IjkGrid` isolate with its crops) and left the rest behind, and nobody saw a broken button because the tool was GATED on "this view has an IJK grid", so it simply never appeared. Restored by widening the isolate rather than adding a class: the isolate's machinery — `EnergisticsExtractor(ExtractPath=...)`, the chain keyed on `id(upstream)`, `_show_source_or_chain`, `_colourable_sources` — never needed a structured grid; only `ExplicitStructuredGridCrop` did. So `set_node_id` accepts any representation, records `_is_ijk`, and skips crops, volumes and the extent-derived slicer state when it is false; the isolate stays in `full` mode, where `show()` already displays the extractor or its threshold tips. In `boot`, `_threshold_target` replaces `_slicer_grid` for the four threshold controllers (the SLICERS keep `_slicer_grid` — a crop really does need IJK), `ISOLATABLE` names the kinds, and the tool gate moves from `needs="grid"` to `needs="thresholdable"`. Measured on ALK_14Layers (unstructured, 75 460 cells): the composite lets the block go, a visible `Threshold` proxy is coloured by `region`, and the toolbar offers `thresholds` while correctly withholding `slicers`. Headless: `threshold_unstructured.json`, whose probe asserts BOTH the drawn colour and that the card is offered — a chain that works behind a hidden button is not a feature.
- **A thresholded grid follows the property eye** (2026-09-23, `ijkgrid._colourable_sources`): with a chain active the isolate does NOT show its base sources — `_show_source_or_chain` hides them and shows the chain's TIP proxies instead — but every colouring loop only walked the base sources. So changing which property the eye colours moved the composite and left the one VISIBLE display on the previous array: measured on drogon, the isolate's own title said `Temperature`, the composite said `Temperature`, and the visible Threshold proxy still said `Facies`. The five display loops (colour, representation type, Z scale, hide) now go through `_colourable_sources()`, which adds the chain's proxies. Headless: `threshold_colour_probe` in `threshold_eye.json` and `threshold_eye_volve.json`, which read what the SHOWN representations name rather than what the isolate believes.
- **A constant field says so on its bar** (2026-09-23, `pdc_scene`): colouring by an array every cell shares paints the whole object ONE flat colour, the middle of the map — which reads exactly like "the grid has lost its colours", and is neither a failure nor the SolidColor grey. The scene already knew the case (`_widened` opens a zero-width range to (v-0.5, v+0.5) so the constant sits mid-bar) but nothing on screen said it. `_block_range` is now `_widened` over a new `_block_raw_range`, and the bar's title carries `— constant <value>` when the raw union has no width. Found on Volve's `active_cell` (1 everywhere) after a reader spent an hour taking it for a bug; drogon's well channel `Coal Facies Indicator` (constant 0) is the same case. `block_color_probe` now FAILS when a block is painted from a constant array and no bar says so.
- **The crosshair no longer stutters** (2026-09-23, `boot.cursor_hit`): on a plain 3D view the point under the pointer came from `ConvertDisplayToPointOnSurface`, which is a hardware SELECTION render, not a ray cast — profiled on the real gesture on Volve, 435 to 1 073 ms per sample, twice that when the block name was bought by a second `SelectSurfaceCells`, while the client sent one every 160 ms; the single-threaded server delivered no frame while it paid, which is what "not fluid" was. Now a camera ray (`camera.view_ray`) is met with a `vtkStaticCellLocator` per DRAWN dataset, cached by (address, MTime): built once (100 ms on 930 000 cells, said in the log), then 3 ms a sample. The ray is brought to data space by undoing the composite's Z exaggeration; an isolated block is hit on what its isolate has visible (crops, tips), never on a cell a threshold hid; the block name is the locator that hit, so `_block_under` and the client's "settled shot" flag are gone. Same point to the centimetre as the selection gave. `CURSOR_MS` drops from 160 to 60. Headless: `profile_cursor.json` (the per-sample profile), `cursor_readout.json`, `cursor_globe.json`.
- **The crosshair, and why it has two roads** (2026-09-23, `boot._cursor_pos`): a **plain 3D** view is answered by ParaView's surface pick (`ConvertDisplayToPointOnSurface`), which gives the point on the DATA; a **Globe** view is answered by a camera ray (`camera.view_ray`) met with the WGS84 ellipsoid in closed form (`geo.ellipsoid_hit`), which gives the point on the GROUND and says so. The split is not a preference: `ConvertDisplayToPointOnSurface` reads as a ray cast but in ParaView runs a hardware surface SELECTION, and on a Globe scene **it never returns** — measured 2026-09-23 on the GPU and in software alike, the session froze with no readout, no frames and no state, which is what "the cursor position does not work at all" was. The depth buffer is no third road: outside a render it is not the current frame (its clipping range belonged to an older one), and at local-CRS ranges the whole scene reads back crushed between 0.9968 and 1.0. The VTK block name is bought from a second surface selection and therefore only on the **settled** shot — the client marks it with a fifth element on `pos_ev__<vid>`, 1 for the trailing one — and it goes FIRST, because asking for the point first left that selection finding nothing at the very pixel the point came from. Headless: `cursor_probe` in `cursor_readout.json` (plain 3D, block name asserted) and `cursor_globe.json`.
- **The wheel stops at the data** (2026-09-22, `app/views/camera.py`): the wheel used to dolly the eye toward the focal point without asking what was in the way, so a few notches too many put the camera inside the grid and then out the other side, on a blank frame with no clue how to get back. `install_wheel_wall(vid, pv_view, state)` hangs a `WheelWall` on the view's interactor, one observer on either side of the style's own handler (priority `1.0` / `-1.0`): the notch is let through, and if it landed the eye inside the box the visible props occupy — padded by `MARGIN_RATIO` (2 %) of its diagonal — the position the camera had before it is put back. The step is **refused, not corrected**: snapping the eye onto the face would move it in a gesture meant to be blocked, and from inside the box — where a reset or a growing dataset can legitimately leave it — it would teleport; a camera already inside is therefore left alone. The refusal is written on the view's bottom-right overlay (`ui_zoom_note__<vid>`), and the first notch outwards takes it back off (`MouseWheelBackwardEvent` → `stepped_out`). On a **Globe** view the box is taken from the DATA's own representations (`visible_box(data_only=True)`: the visible ones the scene has not marked `Pickable = 0`, which is how globe_scene tells the Earth and the wellhead marks from the data), read off `rep.Input.GetDataInformation()` — a representation proxy has none of its own, and in this application every actor reports VTK's empty bounds. It used to be SKIPPED there, on the reading that the box was the planet: it was, and that is exactly why nothing ever stopped — the camera is always already inside a cube twelve thousand kilometres across, so one could fly straight through the grid on the Earth (2026-09-23). The title bar's zoom notches (`nav_zoom_step`) stop at the same wall and read from the same line — two controls for one gesture must not disagree about where the data begins. Headless: `zoom_wall_probe` in `zoom_wall.json` and `zoom_wall_globe.json` (220 notches, the eye moved 25 522 km and stopped outside a 17.6 km box).

## 5. Adding a panel or a card

1. **The widget**: copy the layout of an existing one (`widgets/globe` for a card, `widgets/reconcile` for a panel) — `pyproject.toml`, `trame_fespp_<x>/__init__.py` (`__version__`), `module/__init__.py` (`serve`, `scripts`, `styles`, `vue_use`), `widgets/<x>_panel.py` (the `AbstractElement` wrapper with `_attr_names` and `_event_names`, kebab-case tuples for a multi-word event), `vue-components/` (`package.json`, `vite.config.js` with the UMD name, `src/main.js`, `src/<X>.vue`). Copy `node_modules` from another widget (the build is offline), `npm run build`, commit the bundle under `module/serve` when the widget's `.gitignore` keeps it.
2. **Write it in the charter's components** (`widgets/_shared/components`): `fp-panel` for a session panel, `fp-btn` / `fp-select` / `fp-seg` / `fp-num` / `fp-chip` / `fp-note` / `fp-empty` / `fp-export`. The eslint gate (`sh widgets/lint.sh`, `widgets/.eslintrc.widgets.json`) refuses a raw `<button>`, `<select>`, `<input type="number">`, an `-empty` class or a private copy of a shared class; there is no pending list to hide behind.
3. **The mount**: a `_build_<tool>` in `tools_config.py` plus its `TOOLS` dict (a card), or `app/views/<kind>_panel.py` plus its `VIEW_KINDS` entry and a top-bar button in `__main__.py` (a panel). Bind every prop to a state var and every event to a controller that names the key and says what it refuses.
4. **The module, eagerly**: add the module to `_modules` in `__main__.py` AND the package to `setup/apps.yml` — or the client never receives the bundle and the tag renders blank ("components Vue could not resolve" in the browser harness).
5. **The state**: a per-view var goes into `per_view_state.SUFFIXED_VARS` / `DICT_BUCKETS`; a per-panel key list is shared by the seeding site and `on_panel_closed`.
6. **The tests**: a browser spec (`tests/browser/specs`, the 04c sweep reads every panel's invariants for free), a headless probe in `tests/visual/runner/scenario.py` when the feature can be checked without a browser, a unit test for the engine module. Then `.\test-unit.ps1`, `.\test-browser.ps1`, and a human look on the control container (`.\_local\browser-check.ps1`).

## Depth and time per 3D view (28–29/10)

A 3D view has its own vertical exaggeration: the title bar's **Z ×** (`Title3dView`, prop `zScale`, event `set-zscale`), an `FpRange` from 1 to 50 and an `FpNum` for any value up to 100, state `ui_scale_z__<vid>` (see [[Architecture]], Z scale).

The card's code: `app/features/time_depth_law.py` (the state, the gestures, the zone), `time_depth_prefills.py` (the Pre-fill), `time_depth_models.py` (below), `time_depth_tree.py` (the grid in the tree), `time_depth_pins.py` (the columns in the view).

**A document restored over the session that saved it.** The version the tree holds stays the model when its grid has the saved uuid and its cube the same signature: nothing is written. Over a session that has moved on, the saved uuids are taken, so the model is written under new ones, numbered after the versions the document replaced (the reader skips a file name it has read), and `session_host._under_models_uuids` rewrites the document with `time_depth_tree_renamed()` before reading its selection and its views.

**A velocity model of the data, as it is** (the PO, 12/11; `time_depth_models._grid_as_is`): a lattice grid the editable model cannot hold (`_as_is_needed`: > `MAX_CELLS` on an axis, columns not along X/Y, vertical axis I or J — read on the nodes, `_axes_of`) is read at full resolution into a `velocity_model.Cube` with a `frame` (its first node and the steps of one column along its two horizontal axes; `cells_of` inverts it), float32, depths integrated in place; the entry holds `data` = its representation path, is never written to the tree (`Models.listing` leaves it out), has its values made ready in place by `velocity_model.read_velocities` (a cell with no velocity takes the nearest one of its column, `fill_missing`; RMS velocities become interval ones; a grid that starts away from time 0 is given the depth of its top, its first velocity carried on up to time 0 — the note says each), is named in a session's document by `data` and `velocity` (the property's path) and read again by `ctx.time_depth_law_restore_data`, which `session_host` calls after its reads (`time_depth_models.again`), refuses the card's gestures, has no TDLaw (`law.data`: the card hides the tracks and the Pre-fill row and shows `law.note`; `ctx.time_depth_columns` empty, so no pins). Its velocity is the property the card's list names, and that list alone (the PO, 13/11: the eye no longer chooses it — looking at another property made it the velocity); until the list says one, `_velocity_of` takes one named as a velocity, else the first. A property typed anew is read again (`ctx.property_kind_listeners`). No grid is ever taken unasked (the manager, 13/11): the session's own model stays the active one until a grid is chosen in the card's **3D grid** list, which offers every lattice IJK grid of the data (`_candidate`); any lattice grid in time is read as it is (`_from_grid`). A velocity model of the data ticked in an empty view opens it in time and is never converted (`view_domain._velocity_models`). **Property kinds** (`app/features/property_types`, `engine/property_types`): set in the T→D card's **Average velocity** row, *from* **3D property**: the property (`{op: velocity_property, path}` → `time_depth_models.pick_property`; `law.velocity` {path, name, kind, at, reading, properties}) *of kind* Average | Interval | RMS velocity (`{op: velocity_kind, reading}` → `time_depth_law.READ_AS`), handled before a data model's refusal; `{op: velocity_at, at}` is still answered but the card shows no *Value at* (the manager, 13/11: out for now, top by default) — the tree's right-click "Type…" was taken out the same evening (the PO); `property_kinds` {uuid: kind} and `property_positions` {uuid: top|centre|bottom} kept in the session (OpenAPI too); `ctx.property_kind_of(node)` = the kind set, else the file's `propertyKind`; `ctx.property_position_of(node)`, top by default (`Cube(at=…)`). Opening the model's view turns the 3D view in use to time (`view_doors.open_time_depth`). A view in time never converts depth to time (the manager, 12/11: `view_domain` converts in a depth view alone). `velocity_reading(kind)`: interval (and a bare "velocity"), average (`avg seismic` / `avg acoustic velocity`: `Cube(average=True)`, depth = v·t/2 at each cell's top — the time of the seismic sample it was (the PO, 12/11) —, the last carried to the bottom, interval velocities derived in place), rms (made interval velocities by Dix's formula, not smoothed: `velocity_model.interval_from_rms`).

**No default model (16/11).** A session starts with NO velocity model: `Models.current` is None, the law's memory holds the inputs of one to generate (`law.generated` false, `law.model` "new"), `_rebuild` registers no cube and `view_domain` converts nothing — a time object asked for in a view in depth stays undrawn and `view_domain._say_no_model` poses the shared box (`no_model_question`: open the model's view, switch the view to time, cancel), from a tick (`on_tick`), a switch to depth (`view_domain`) or a click on a T tag. `{op: generate}` (`time_depth_law._generate`, the view's **Generate**, offered while `law.can_generate`) makes "Velocity model N" (`Models.generate`, `numbered`) of the inputs once a TDLaw converts and a zone can be told; `engine/time_depth.Model` starts with no column and has no default law (`has_law`); a TDLaw entered by hand starts on `starting_law`, a line at 2 000 m/s to edit. ## The whole scene cut (29/10)

The Geometry slicer cuts the **whole scene** of a 3D view when no object is picked in its list nor active (`geometry_slicer._target`: the pin "@scene", else `Activation.target_of(..., fallback=False)`, else the scene; the
first gesture on the scene's card pins "@scene", so an active node clicked
afterwards does not take the card off a scene still cut; "Whole scene" heads the card's list and its title). Its subjects are X, Y, Z over the bounds of what the view draws (`_scene_bounds`, the union of `_rep_bounds` of the shown blocks); a range that held the whole scene keeps holding it as objects are read (`slicer_card.follow_extents`, every card). The rule is the object slicer's: a cell is kept when it meets a box. The card is drawn by `PdcScene.set_crop` on ONE pipeline per view (`engine/blockscope/scene_crop`), whatever the number of boxes, on the shared source with its blocks and their numbering kept: once per grid — `FesppScenePrep`, each cell's id, an IJK grid made the unstructured grid it is (an explicit structured grid's shallow copy costs 30–45 ms, and each stage after makes some) with its face table (which faces between I, J, K neighbours are one face, which are lone), the lines and points cells of their own (a well, a log channel, a point set is one cell in the plugin's output: kept whole otherwise), every cell's extent — a horizon's read off its point grid (the PO, 29/10 evening: a horizon ticked on a cut scene showed whole) — then at each step `FesppSceneCut`: the cells a box meets in numpy, the IJK grid drawn as the polydata of its exposed faces straight from the table (a shared face with one side kept, a lone face of a kept cell: no cell copied, no surface for ParaView to extract), the wells and horizons as their kept cells in numpy. The two filters are Python VTK algorithms loaded as a ParaView plugin (`scene_cut_filters.py`): a ProgrammableFilter collects Python's garbage after every run (33 ms of the app's heap). The blocks are not extracted: an `ExtractBlock` renumbers them, and a log ticked on a cut scene crashed the session (29/10, scenario `etp_scene_cut_logs`). Measured on drogon's 930 000 cells, a step end to end (the cut, the representation, the render): 66 ms for a box narrowed on its three axes, 86 ms for two boxes, 83 ms for a step of the player — against 98, 179 and 118 ms with ParaView's Threshold and surface filter (the PO, 29/10 evening: "less slow, still too slow"), the rotation 6–8 ms a frame; the first version (a Clip a plane, a pipeline and a representation a box) cost 170–210 ms here and grew with every plane — the PO measured 1.5 s a step on ADME. A drag is paced by the live coalescer (`slicer_player.Coalescer`, the latest wish drawn, `IDLE_S` = 50 ms left for the frame to go out). With many objects shown (the PO, 29/10 evening: "as soon as representations are added it slows down a lot"), a step costs 60 ms with drogon's 12 wells (26 representations), 100 ms on ADME with the grid, 18 trajectories and 6 horizons — the cut itself 22 / 50 ms, ParaView's representation 7 ms, the render 25 ms; what grew with the objects was the app's own flush, `exact_colours._apply_flat_colors` reading two properties of every representation through paraview.simple's wrappers at every draw (25 ms a gesture with 12 wells): each representation is now lit once, on its own properties. A scene of 26 representations draws in 30 ms a frame whatever the cut (the wells' heads and labels). Scenarios `scene_cut_many`, `etp_scene_cut_many`. A pick on the cut scene comes back to the source's cell (`vtkOriginalCellIds`) and block (the same flat index; `picking._crop_to_source`). An object drawn by its own isolate (an object slicer or a Property slicer on it) is cut too, since 29/10 evening (the PO: "the whole scene does nothing" on a grid a card had isolated): `IjkGrid.set_scene_cut` puts a prep → cut stage (the same filters) between each display of the isolate and what it drew, the look inherited, the colour followed, the cuts dropped when the scene is whole; `geometry_slicer.draw` drives them with the composite, and an isolate made while the scene is cut is cut at birth (`ctx.scene_cut_for_isolate`). Measured on ALK (75 460 unstructured cells isolated by a Property slicer): the scene narrowed to its middle half → 21 728 cells on the isolate, a frame 6 ms. Known limits: the wellhead and its name stay; a selection made before the cut does not show on the cut scene. On a grid of steeply dipping layers, a cut "=" keeps the whole layer the plane crosses (the rule: a cell is kept when it meets the box — on ALK's 14 layers, 5 760 cells spanning 700 m), and "≥" every cell reaching above the plane: the rule, not a fault (the PO, 29/10 evening). Scenarios `scene_slicer` (drogon: the grid, a well, its markers), `scene_cut_perf` and `etp_scene_cut_perf` (the timings, ADME with `OSDU_TOKEN`), `scene_cut_logs` and `etp_scene_cut_logs` (a log ticked while the scene is cut), `scene_cut_faces_check` (the grid's exposed faces against a hash of every face of its kept cells).
